Euler characteristic is a topological invariant that describes the topological space's shape \cite{eulerWikipedia}.
For polyhedra it is defined as 



$$\chi = V - E + F$$



where $V,E$ and $F$ are the number of vertices, edges and faces, respectively.

According to the [Hairy ball theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hairy_ball_theorem), the sum of vortices of a vector field over a sphere equals two, connecting to the polarisation vortices ([BIC topological charge]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _local_charges/bic_charge.md %})) in the far-field radiation of a finite-size structure.
Additionally, according to the same theorem, the sum of vortices over a torus equals zero.

This gives us an apparent connection between the [BIC topological charge]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _local_charges/bic_charge.md %}) and the Euler characteristic



$$\chi = \sum_{i} q(\mathbf{k}_i)$$
